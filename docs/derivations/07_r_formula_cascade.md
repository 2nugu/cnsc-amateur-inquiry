# Derivation 7 — r Formula in Non-Inflationary Context (cascade closure)

**Date**: 2026-05-13
**Purpose**: Test whether $r = 16\,\epsilon_{\text{eff}}\,c_s\,(\beta/\alpha)$ has a meaningful derivation in CNSC, after the cascade of weaknesses 1 and 2.

## 1. Cascade context

The standard inflationary formula $r = 16\epsilon c_s$ comes from:
- $P_\zeta = H^2 / (8\pi^2 \epsilon c_s M_P^2)$, with $\zeta$ frozen at horizon exit.
- $P_h = 2 H^2 / (\pi^2 M_P^2)$, with $h$ frozen at horizon exit.
- $r = P_h / P_\zeta = 16\epsilon c_s$.

In CNSC after weakness 2 closure:
- **There is no horizon-exit freezing**.  Neither $\zeta$ nor $h$ acquires a constant super-Hubble value in $w=+1$ background.
- Therefore $P_\zeta$ and $P_h$ are *not well-defined* as the cosmologically observable amplitudes that inflation produces.

The standard $r = 16\epsilon c_s$ derivation **does not apply**.

## 2. What about an alternative imprinting mechanism?

The CNSC paper's analogue formula $r = 16\epsilon_{\text{eff}} c_s (\beta/\alpha)$ from Coupled Perturbations §6 uses:
- $\epsilon_{\text{eff}} = -\dot H/H^2 = 3$ in stiff matter — a *defined* quantity, OK.
- $c_s$ as the constant from Derivation 1 — OK.
- $\beta/\alpha$ hierarchy — OK.

But the formula is derived *by analogy* with the inflationary case, not from first principles.  The key step assumed is that *some* imprinting mechanism produces $P_h \propto H^2/M_P^2 \cdot (\beta/\alpha)$ and the analogous $P_\zeta$ — but weakness 2 showed no such mechanism exists.

## 3. Alternative: thermal $r$ from shear correlations

One can attempt a non-inflationary derivation: if the *thermal* shear correlations $\langle\delta\sigma^2\rangle$ at the critical point are imprinted on cosmological tensor modes (analogous to thermal field theory of cosmic strings), one obtains

$$P_h^{\text{thermal}} \sim \frac{T_c^2}{M_P^2}\cdot\frac{\beta}{\alpha}$$

But there is no analogous *thermal* $P_\zeta$ — the scalar perturbation is set by Kibble-Zurek defect density (weakness 6 cascade), which is *not* a Gaussian spectrum with a defined $P_\zeta$.

So the ratio $r = P_h/P_\zeta$ is *not* well-defined cosmologically in CNSC.

## 4. Verdict

| Intuition | Status |
|---|---|
| I-11 (shear coupling produces small but nonzero $r$) | ❌ **Cascade not defensible** — no well-defined $P_\zeta$ to take the ratio with; cosmological $r$ as an observable is not produced |

## 5. The "small $r$" claim revisited

The CNSC paper's *qualitative* claim — *"CNSC predicts much smaller $r$ than inflation"* — survives in the following weaker form:

> "Whatever cosmological $r$ CNSC produces (if any), it is suppressed by the $\beta/\alpha$ hierarchy of the null kinetic action."

This is *defensible at the parametric level* but produces no numerical prediction.  In particular, it does *not* yield the windows $[10^{-7}, 10^{-5}]$ or $[10^{-10}, 10^{-5}]$ that the paper claims — those windows come from the *quantitative* formula that has been shown not to apply.

## 6. Closure of weakness 3

The $r$ formula T3.7 is **not derivable** in CNSC under the cascade of weaknesses 1 and 2.  It is an analogy from inflation that does not survive the absence of horizon-exit freezing.

Intuition I-11: ❌ Not defensible.
